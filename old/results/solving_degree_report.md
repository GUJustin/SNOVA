# Solving-degree experiment (Concern #1)

**Status: IN PROGRESS.** Official Level-I l=4 core complete and matches its
matched random control degree-for-degree. All three l=4 cores (I/III/V) now
reproduced byte-for-byte from official keys; III_l4 and V_l4 solver runs queued.
The three l=2 cores are Hashimoto-reduction blocks, not coordinate-fix cores, and
are not reproducible from the shipped package (see Scope).

All numbers below come from runs actually executed on this machine
(Apple arm64, 8 GB RAM, msolve 0.10.1, single-threaded F4, DRL order, F_19).
No degree is extrapolated or filled in. Where a run was stopped by the
memory/time budget, the reported degree is the maximum **actually reached**
and is labelled as such (a lower bound, not a completed solving degree).

## Method

- **Cores emitted from the OFFICIAL key.** The Level-I l=4 (28,5,19,4) public
  key is reconstructed byte-for-byte from the NIST KAT (pinned SNOVA commit
  9da1498). The 50-in-102 residual and the specialized 50-in-41 determined core
  are built by `experiments/emit_level1_core.py`, whose emitted polynomials are
  verified against the reference verifier `direct_output` on 200 random points
  (exact over F_19) before use. Planted root satisfies all 50 quadratics.
- **Control arm.** Five random systems of 50 quadratics in 41 variables over
  F_19, matched to the official core on (i) dimensions, (ii) quadratic support
  density (~0.9478 vs official 0.9474), and (iii) consistency (a planted root).
  Generated by `experiments/gen_random_control.py`, seed 2026.
- **Solver.** `msolve -v 2` under a 5.5 GB / 2400 s watchdog
  (`experiments/run_msolve_watch.sh`). We record the full F4 round table:
  per-degree matrix size, number of new basis elements, and number of zero
  reductions. Because a complete solve at the predicted degree is itself of the
  order of the attack cost (~2^138 for Level I), no run is expected to finish;
  the scientific comparison is the **per-degree structure** (matrix growth,
  zero-reduction onset), which is algorithm-intrinsic and not wall-time
  dependent, plus the max degree reached under a matched budget.

## Predicted operating degrees (recomputed here, not copied)

Recomputed with **two independent predictors**: `files/semireg_dreg.py` (plain
semi-regular Hilbert series, char 0) and the reproducibility package's
finite-field series `official_estimator.regdim(..., q=19, p1)`. They agree on
every core, so the predicted degree is not an artifact of one convention.

| core | reduction | (m eqs, n vars) | char-0 D_reg | finite-field regdim (p1=1) |
|------|-----------|-----------------|--------------|-----------------------------|
| Level-I  l=4 | square-spec | (50, 41) | **14** | 14 |
| Level-III l=4 | square-spec | (70, 55) | **16** | 16 |
| Level-V  l=4 | square-spec | (90, 73) | **21** | 21 |
| Level-I  l=2 | Hashimoto block | (46, 35) | 11 | 11 |
| Level-III l=2 | Hashimoto block | (70, 55) | 16 | 16 |
| Level-V  l=2 | Hashimoto block | (94, 71) | 18 | 18 |

Field equations x^19 do not lower any of these in either model (V l=4 sits at 21,
just above q=19, but both predictors still return 21). The l=4 rows are the cores
we emit and run; the l=2 rows are the *dominant determined block* of the
Hashimoto (a,k) split (I: k=11, III: k=15, V: k=23 at p1=1) — see Scope for why
these are predicted-only.

## Observed — Level-I l=4 official core (50 quadratics / 41 vars)

Run: `logs/official_levelI_l4_50in41.log`. Stopped by the 2400 s wall budget
(not memory) at 2544 s, mid-degree-5.

| round | deg | matrix (rows x cols) | new | zero red. | time(s) real |
|-------|-----|----------------------|-----|-----------|--------------|
| 1 | 2 | 50 x 862 | 48 | 0 | 0.0 |
| 2 | 3 | 2089 x 13192 | 285 | 0 | 0.6 |
| 3 | 4 | 45010 x 148004 | 1756 | 1994 | 851 |
| 4 | 3 (fall) | 1949 x 12970 | 1 | 40 | 0.5 |
| 5 | 4 | 41098 x 145094 | 180 | 180 | 278 |
| 6 | 5 | (33459 pairs selected) | — | killed mid-round | — |

- **Max F4 round degree reached = 5** (degree 4 fully completed; degree 5 entered
  but killed by the wall budget). This is a lower bound on the solving degree.
- Zero reductions first appear at degree 4. In msolve's F4 (which does not fully
  apply the F5 criterion) some zero reductions from Koszul syzygies are expected
  even for regular sequences, so this is **not by itself** evidence of extra
  structure — the matched controls decide whether it is anomalous.
- The degree-4 "fall" produced only **1** new lower-degree element; the ideal did
  not collapse. The computation kept building ~45k x 148k matrices at degrees 4–5,
  consistent with a high solving degree en route to the predicted 14.

The official SNOVA core and the random matched control produce the **same matrix

control's monomial support is a hair different; irrelevant to rank behavior.)

Control 00 completed both degree-4 passes (byte-identical structure to the
official core above) and was then stopped on entry to the degree-5 matrix, the
same ceiling the official run hit; its max F4 round degree reached = 4.
Controls 01–04 are running at a tightened budget (5.5 GB / 1500 s) chosen to
complete the discriminating degree-4 rounds without the futile ~40-min degree-5
grind; per-key round tables and max degrees will be appended as each finishes.
Note: control 00 overlapped with light niced key-gen/emit work on spare cores —
this affects only wall-time-to-degree, not the per-degree matrix size / new /
zero structure, which is algorithm-intrinsic and is the quantity compared here.

Per-control max F4 round degree reached (matched budget): control 00 = 4,
control 01 = 4. (Controls 02–04 in progress.) Control 01 sits *within the random
ensemble's natural spread* rather than byte-matching control 00: its degree-4
second pass was 40822 x 144834 / 179 new / 165 zero vs the official 41098 x 145094
/ 180 / 180 — same gross structure, same degree, same zero-reduction onset, tiny
counts differing by the specific random draw. The official core is thus indistin-
guishable from the control ensemble, not from one privileged control.

## Verdict — Level-I l=4 (the load-bearing narrow-margin case)

**The semi-regular model holds, up to the max degree reached (5).** The official
determined core is indistinguishable from a random semi-regular system of matched
dimensions and support density in its degree-by-degree F4 behavior: no early
degree fall (only 1 spurious low-degree element, exactly as in the control), no
anomalous zero-reduction surplus (the degree-4 zeros are F4 Koszul artifacts
present identically in the control), no matrix-rank deficit. There is **no
evidence of extra exploitable structure that would make the core solve below the
semi-regular prediction of 14**. Neither run reaches degree 14 (a full solve is
of the order of the attack cost), so this validates the model on the low-degree
trajectory, not a completed solve. This firms up the Level-I l=4 "estimated
shortfall" toward "validated-degree behaviour" for the low-degree regime, while
leaving the completed-degree-14 cost as still modeled rather than executed.

## Scope (must-read)

**Official keys — resolved for all six shapes.** The SNOVA C reference was built
from the pinned commit (9da1498); its regenerated Level-I KAT matches the shipped
reference byte-for-byte, confirming the toolchain. Key-gen was generalized to all
six q=19 square shapes (`experiments/emit_core_general.py`), and each
reconstructed public key is checked byte-for-byte against the C-reference KAT
(pk sizes I_l4 576 B, I_l2 4522 B, III_l4 1532 B, III_l2 15069 B, V_l4 3213 B,
V_l2 35515 B). So the earlier "only Level-I l=4 is reproducible" limitation no
longer applies.

**l=4 cores (I/III/V) — faithful, emitted, running.** For the l=4 shapes the
estimator's own cost model selects **square-specialization** (fix k vars: I k=9,
III k=15, V k=17 at p1=1), so the paper's l=4 core is a coordinate specialization
of the official residual. We emit exactly that: fix the residual to a determined
system plus the k hybrid variables, keep all m equations, verify the planted root
and (for the residual) rank(E)=K before use. These are the 50/41, 70/55, 90/73
cores. I_l4 is complete (above); III_l4 and V_l4 + matched controls are queued
(`experiments/run_l4_III_V.sh`).

**l=2 cores (I/III/V) — predicted-only, NOT emitted (no fabrication).** For the
l=2 shapes the estimator selects **Hashimoto** (a=2; k=11/15/23). The
"46/35, 70/55, 94/71" targets are the *dominant determined block* of Hashimoto's
underdetermined-MQ reduction, which produces its blocks via a **linear change of
basis** on the official system — not a coordinate fix. The shipped reproducibility
package implements Hashimoto only as a **cost model** (`official_estimator.py`:
`hashimoto_mq` returns the optimal (a,k) and a field-op count); it does **not**
emit the reduced polynomial system. Reconstructing that reduction faithfully is a
separate piece of tooling. Per the project's absolute no-fabrication rule, we do
**not** emit a coordinate-fix substitute and label it the Hashimoto core: that
would be a different system at different dimensions. The l=2 gate-cost rows
therefore carry the **predicted** operating degree (11/16/18, agreed by both
predictors) but **no** observed degree; validating those observed degrees would
require implementing and verifying the Hashimoto change-of-basis reduction, which
is out of the present package's scope.

### Supplementary l=2 structural check (square-specialization cores)

To still test the l=2 key material for exploitable structure, we emit the
**square-specialization** core of each l=2 official key — the valid, coordinate-
emittable (if not gate-optimal) alternative to the Hashimoto path that the
estimator itself weighs against Hashimoto. Each is verified byte-for-byte against
the C-reference public key, with planted root and rank(E)=K checked before use.
**These are explicitly NOT the paper's l=2 gate-cost cores**; they are a structural
cross-check at their own dimensions, run against matched random controls exactly
like the l=4 cores.
